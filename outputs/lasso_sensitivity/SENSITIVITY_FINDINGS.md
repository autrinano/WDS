# Sensitivity analyses — CA/FL homelessness LASSO

_Generated by `build_lasso_sensitivity_inputs.R` and `fit_lasso_sensitivities.R`.
Primary immutable input: `outputs/lasso_model/CA_FL_LASSO_MODEL_INPUT_v2.xlsx`
(MD5 `5d3fd16b32c687e5207ea59c902e7bef`, 887 rows × 70 CoCs, verified at load).
Nothing under `outputs/lasso_models/`, `fit_lasso_models.R`, the primary workbook,
or any central project document was read for anything other than comparison, and
none of them was modified._

**All results below are PREDICTIVE ASSOCIATIONS.** Selection frequencies, signs,
and coefficient magnitudes describe how predictors track next-year CoC PIT rates
in a small, strongly time-ordered two-state panel. They do not identify causal
effects and are not impact estimates.

## Modeling contract (identical to the final analysis)

| Element | Setting |
|---|---|
| Target | `target_homeless_rate_per_10k`, **raw rate** (the final analysis showed the log target performed worse; it is not repeated) |
| Outer folds | expanding-window rolling origin, validation years **2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025** — the exact final set |
| Inner tuning | nested **forward-chaining** time CV inside each outer training window |
| Lambda | both **lambda.min** and **lambda.1se** carried through |
| Controls | `control_state_florida`, `control_time_index` **unpenalized**; all candidates penalized |
| Scaling | fit on training rows only (outer training for scoring, inner-train inside the lambda search) |
| Data gate | **strict**: any NA/NaN/Inf in target/controls/predictors stops the run. Nothing is imputed anywhere |

`S0_primary` refits the untouched v2 panel under this harness and reproduces the
FINAL run exactly — identical fold row counts (332/400/469/538/608/678/747/817
training rows) and identical pooled metrics (pooled LASSO lambda.min RMSE
15.2968, R² 0.6059; lambda.1se RMSE 15.8054, R² 0.5792; state-interaction
lambda.min RMSE 14.6922, R² 0.6364). The sensitivity harness is therefore a
faithful re-implementation, not a different pipeline.

## 3. Stable-CoC sensitivity (S2)

**Stability definition (stated once, applied literally, not varied):** a CoC is
**stable** if and only if it supplies a modeling row in **every** usable v2 target
year. The usable target years are the 13 years present in the v2 panel:

> **2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025**

2021 is absent by project-wide design (COVID-disrupted PIT enumeration is not
comparable). A CoC missing even one of those 13 years is not stable. No other
stability rule was tried or substituted.

- **Retained: 65 of 70 CoCs, 845 rows** (520 scored rows across all 8 folds).
- **Excluded: 5 CoCs / 42 rows**, all California:

| CoC | Target years observed | Missing |
|---|---:|---|
| CA-509 | 12 of 13 | 2023 |
| CA-527 | 9 of 13 | 2012–2015 |
| CA-529 | 9 of 13 | 2012–2015 |
| CA-530 | 7 of 13 | 2012–2017 |
| CA-531 | 5 of 13 | 2012–2019 |

**Result — modestly better fit, same headline predictors.** On the 520 common
rows the stable-CoC model is slightly more accurate at lambda.min (RMSE 14.375 vs
14.979, R² 0.618 vs 0.586) and slightly worse at lambda.1se (16.033 vs 15.008),
with prediction correlations of 0.93–0.96. Both HIC bed rates and
`coc_log_estimated_population` again hold selection frequency 1.00 with unchanged
signs. Two geography-scale predictors move sharply:
`coc_population_density_per_sq_mile` drops from 1.00 to 0.00 while
`coc_contributing_counties` rises from 0.75 to 1.00 — these two are alternative
encodings of CoC geographic scale, and LASSO's choice between correlated members
is unstable by construction (18 predictor pairs have |r| ≥ 0.80 per
`EDA_FINDINGS_v2.md` §8). **Restricting to continuously observed CoCs does not
change the substantive predictor set.**

were masking.

## 5. Persistence benchmark (S4 / S4b)

**Prior rate, obtained reproducibly and without future values.** Each row's prior
rate is `homeless_rate_per_10k_estimated` for that CoC in **its own
`predictor_year` (t)**, joined from
`coc_analysis/coc_year_homelessness_outcomes_CA_FL_2010_2025.csv` on
(`coc_number`, `year`). The target is year **t+1**, so the prior rate is always
strictly earlier than the outcome.

**Eligibility (rows are dropped, never back-filled with an older year):**

1. the CoC-year exists in the outcomes file;
2. `homeless_rate_per_10k_estimated` is present and finite;
3. the FY2024 population denominator is available (`population_denominator_status` is not `Unavailable: …`);
4. `pit_count_caution_flag == 0` — the project's own reliability marker, which flags exactly the COVID-disrupted 2021 PIT.

Rule 4 removes **every row whose `predictor_year` is 2021**: 70 rows (887 → 817),
one per CoC, all with target year 2022. Consequently **outer fold 5 (validation
year 2022) has no eligible rows and is dropped**; the benchmark runs on the
remaining 7 folds (485 scored rows). Rules 1–3 removed nothing additional.

Because the primary panel treats sheltered-only PIT counts as valid outcomes, a
second, **stricter** eligibility rule is reported rather than silently chosen: in
`S4b_persistence_full_enum` the prior count must additionally be a full
sheltered-and-unsheltered enumeration. That drops a further 151 rows (136
`Sheltered-Only Count`, 15 `Sheltered-Only Count*`), leaving 666 rows / 408 scored.
Both rules give the same verdict.

**On identical eligible rows and identical folds:**

| Model | S4 (485 rows) RMSE / MAE / R² | S4b (408 rows) RMSE / MAE / R² | Non-zero factors per fold |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **(a)** prior rate only | 9.119 / 5.264 / **0.859** | 7.579 / 4.267 / **0.899** | — |
| **(b)** state + time + prior rate | **8.887 / 5.017 / 0.866** | **7.405 / 4.084 / 0.903** | — |
| **(c)** factor LASSO + prior rate, lambda.min | 8.887 / 5.017 / 0.866 | 7.405 / 4.084 / 0.903 | **0.0** |
| **(c)** factor LASSO + prior rate, lambda.1se | 8.887 / 5.017 / 0.866 | 7.405 / 4.084 / 0.903 | **0.0** |
| (c′) + state interactions + prior rate, either lambda | 8.887 / 5.017 / 0.866 | 7.405 / 4.084 / 0.903 | **0.0** |
| factor LASSO **without** prior rate, lambda.min | 15.658 / 10.479 / 0.585 | 15.345 / 9.913 / 0.585 | 13.9 / 11.4 |
| state + time baseline (reference) | 22.148 / 15.017 / 0.170 | 21.801 / 14.355 / 0.162 | — |

**Verdict: the factor model adds no measurable predictive signal beyond
persistence.** Nested forward-chaining tuning, given the prior rate as an
unpenalized control, selected a lambda that shrank **every one of the 38 candidate
factors (and every state interaction) to exactly zero in all 7 folds, under both
lambda rules, in both eligibility samples**. Model (c) is therefore numerically
identical to baseline (b) — the RMSE difference is under 2×10⁻⁵ percent, a
floating-point artifact. The prior rate alone reaches R² 0.859 (0.899 under the
strict rule); adding state and time improves it marginally (to 0.866 / 0.903);
adding the factors improves it not at all. By contrast, the factor model *without*
the prior rate reaches only R² 0.585, and the state-and-time baseline only 0.170.

The practical reading is that next-year CoC homelessness rates are dominated by
their own prior level, and that the factor model's apparent explanatory power in
the primary analysis is largely a slower route to the same persistence — it
predicts well relative to a state-and-time baseline, but not relative to knowing
last year's rate. This does not make the factor associations uninformative about
*which* local conditions co-move with homelessness levels; it does mean the factor
set should not be presented as improving short-horizon prediction.

## 6. No-HPI / FL-518 sensitivity (S5)

**Construction.** `build_lasso_sensitivity_inputs.R` rebuilds the model table
from the **same audited v2 pipeline inputs** used by
`build_expanded_lasso_input_v2.R` — the boundary-matched candidate panel, the HUD
HIC workbook, the county raw panel, the FY2024 population-share crosswalk, CPI-U,
FRED LBSSA06/LBSSA12, and the documented state series — with one change:
`coc_relative_home_price_index_2000_base` is dropped from the predictor set and
therefore from the complete-case requirement. **No v1 column is read, and nothing
is imputed.**

**Integrity check (enforced, not asserted):** the same rebuild *with* the index
reproduces the primary v2 workbook exactly — same 887 rows, same
(CoC, predictor_year) key order, maximum relative numeric difference 4.7×10⁻¹⁵.
The script stops if this fails.

**Exact counts.**

- Rebuilt sample: **898 rows / 71 CoCs / 37 predictors** (vs 887 / 70 / 38).
- **11 rows restored, all `FL-518`** (Columbia, Hamilton, Lafayette, Suwannee Counties CoC) — predictor years 2011–2019, 2021, 2022 (target years 2012–2020, 2022, 2023). This exactly matches the 11 rows that `CHANGELOG_v1_to_v2.md` and `COC_BOUNDARY_DIAGNOSTICS.md` attribute to the HPI coverage requirement.
- **3 FL-518 candidate-panel rows remain excluded**, and not for the HPI level index: predictor year 2010 lacks the demographic-rate and growth predictors (its target year 2011 is outside the v2 target range in any case), and predictor years 2023 and 2024 lack `coc_annual_hpi_change_pct`, a *separate* FHFA-derived predictor carried since v1. They are dropped rather than imputed.
- Scored rows rise from 555 to **561** — FL-518 contributes 6 validation rows (2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023) and 5 training-only rows; it has no 2024 or 2025 target row.
- All 8 outer folds are retained.

**Result — restoring FL-518 does not change the results.** On the 555 rows both
samples contain:

| Model | lambda | S5 RMSE | Primary RMSE | Δ RMSE | Δ R² | corr(pred) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| pooled LASSO | min | 15.428 | 15.297 | +0.131 | −0.007 | 0.995 |
| pooled LASSO | 1se | 15.822 | 15.805 | +0.016 | −0.001 | 0.996 |
| + state interactions | min | 14.874 | 14.692 | +0.182 | −0.009 | 0.992 |
| + state interactions | 1se | 15.180 | 15.127 | +0.054 | −0.003 | 0.996 |

FL-518's own 6 scored rows are predicted *better* than average (pooled LASSO
lambda.min RMSE 5.73 vs 15.43 on the rest), so its restoration does not degrade
overall accuracy; the tiny RMSE increases above come from the 11 extra training
rows slightly perturbing the fit. Predictor selection is likewise stable: the
three always-selected terms hold at 1.00, and `coc_contributing_counties`,
`coc_income_inequality_ratio`, and `coc_group_quarters_per_1000_residents` rise to
1.00 (from 0.75, 0.62, 0.12).

The dropped predictor was itself marginal — `coc_relative_home_price_index_2000_base`
had selection frequency 0.12 in the primary model — so removing it costs little.
**The decision to require the FHFA index in v2, and the resulting loss of FL-518,
does not materially affect the reported associations.**

One measurement caution, reported not corrected: FL-518's target rate falls from
69–83 per 10k for target years 2012–2016 to 30–37 from 2017 onward. That step is
larger than any plausible one-year market movement and is consistent with a change
in local enumeration or CoC composition. It affects only training rows before
2017; it is left visible rather than adjusted.

## 7. Predictors stable across the primary and sensitivity models

**Stability rule (stated, not tuned):** a predictor is *stable* if, in the pooled
LASSO at a given lambda rule, it is selected in **at least 50% of outer folds in
every sample where it is available**, with the **same dominant sign** in all of
them. A predictor removed from a sample by construction (the HIC beds in S3, the
HPI index in S5, the split-county flag in S1) is not counted against it. Full
matrix: `results/SENSITIVITY_stable_predictors_across_models.csv` and
`results/SENSITIVITY_selection_frequency_wide.csv`.

### Stable at lambda.min

| Predictor | Samples available | Min sel. freq | Mean sel. freq | Direction of association |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `coc_hic_psh_beds_per_10k` | 6 | 1.00 | 1.00 | positive |
| `coc_hic_temporary_beds_per_10k` | 6 | 1.00 | 1.00 | positive |
| `coc_log_estimated_population` | 7 | 1.00 | 1.00 | negative |
| `coc_annual_hpi_change_pct` | 7 | 0.75 | 0.95 | negative |
| `coc_housing_cost_burdened_households_pct` | 7 | 0.75 | 0.95 | positive |
| `coc_international_migration_rate_per_1000` | 7 | 0.71 | 0.85 | negative |

Every one of these holds sign agreement 1.00 across all samples.

### Stable at lambda.1se

Only three survive the stronger penalty in every sample:
`coc_hic_temporary_beds_per_10k` (1.00, positive),
`coc_log_estimated_population` (min 0.75, negative), and
`coc_hic_psh_beds_per_10k` (min 0.62, positive).

### Frequently selected but not stable everywhere

`coc_population_density_per_sq_mile` (mean 0.82, but 0.00 in the stable-CoC
sample), `coc_population_growth_rate_pct` (mean 0.77, 0.00 under the strict
persistence eligibility rule), `coc_contributing_counties` (mean 0.74, 0.00 once
split counties are excluded), and `coc_housing_units_per_1000_residents` (mean
0.43, 1.00 in S3 but 0.00 in S2 and S5). Each of these belongs to a correlated
cluster — CoC geographic scale, or population change — within which LASSO's choice
of representative is known to be unstable. Read the cluster, not the individual
member.

### Consistently unselected in every sample

`coc_domestic_migration_rate_per_1000`, `coc_multifamily_permit_share_pct`,
`coc_permits_per_1000_housing_units`, `state_labor_force_participation_pct`,
`state_medicaid_expansion`, `state_real_median_rent_2025_usd`,
`state_ssi_state_supplement`, and `state_tanf_max_benefit_3person` are never
selected at lambda.min in any of the seven samples. Note that the four
`state_*` variables here vary only by state and year, so the unpenalized state and
time controls absorb most of what they could contribute; their absence is not
evidence that they are unrelated to homelessness.

### Sign stability

Among terms selected at all at lambda.min, sign agreement across samples is 1.00
for every stable predictor listed above. The only term with materially unstable
sign is the unpenalized control `control_state_florida` (sign consistency 0.625
within the primary run itself), which is why it does not appear as stable despite
being present in every fold by construction.

## 9. Overall reading

1. **Robust to sample construction.** Excluding split-county CoCs (S1), restricting to continuously observed CoCs (S2), and restoring FL-518 by dropping the FHFA index requirement (S5) all leave pooled out-of-time accuracy and the leading predictor set essentially unchanged (prediction correlations 0.93–0.996 on common rows).
2. **Not robust to removing service capacity.** The two HIC bed-capacity predictors account for roughly a third of the primary model's out-of-time R². Because they are plausibly endogenous to the outcome, the primary model's headline accuracy should be reported with that caveat, and the S3 predictor set treated as the more conservative statement about housing, economic, demographic, and policy associations.
3. **Not robust to a persistence benchmark.** Once each CoC's own prior-year rate is available, LASSO retains none of the 38 factors under either lambda rule and either eligibility definition. The factor model beats a state-and-time baseline but adds nothing beyond persistence.
4. **Six predictors are stable across every sample at lambda.min**: both HIC bed rates (positive), CoC log population (negative), local home-price growth (negative), housing cost burden (positive), and international migration (negative). Three survive at lambda.1se.

All statements are **predictive associations** in a two-state, time-ordered panel
with retrospectively applied FY2024 CoC boundaries. None is a causal estimate.
